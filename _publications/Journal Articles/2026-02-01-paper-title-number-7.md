Perforating operations conducted in deep-well, ultra-deep-well, deep-sea, and ultra-deep-sea environments are frequently accompanied by intense shock loads generated by combined perforation and well testing systems. These shock loads can severely damage the internal structure of the wellbore, posing significant safety risks. Accurate calculation of perforating shock loads is therefore a fundamental prerequisite for comprehensive wellbore safety evaluation. However, existing calculation approaches primarily rely on empirical formulations derived from explosive mechanics, which often fail to accommodate multi-parameter coupling effects under realistic operational conditions.

To address this limitation, this study develops an Optuna-CNN based proxy model using perforation shock load data generated through numerical simulations. A series of perforation models were constructed to establish a dataset incorporating variations in total explosive weight, initial wellbore pressure, wellbore explosive volume, detonation interval, and formation pressure. The Pearson correlation coefficient was applied to quantify the relationships between these influencing factors and perforating shock loads.

Subsequently, a convolutional neural network (CNN) proxy model was developed, with hyperparameters optimized using the Optuna framework. The relative importance of hyperparameters was further analyzed to enhance model interpretability. The predictive performance of the proposed Optuna-CNN model was compared with three commonly used regression models, namely feedforward neural network (FNN), support vector machine (SVM), and gradient boosted regression (GBR). Evaluation metrics including MAE, MSE, R?, and MAPE demonstrate that the Optuna-CNN model achieves superior performance, with an R? value of 0.9562.

Finally, the model was validated using measured data from two field wells, yielding calculation errors of 3.35% and 3.28%, respectively, which confirms its high predictive accuracy and robustness. This study provides an efficient and reliable computational tool for rapid prediction of perforating shock loads, offering practical support for wellbore safety assessment and perforation parameter optimization in complex deep-energy environments.
